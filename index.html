How the simulator works

For each rally, the simulation uses the current rotation's point scoring percentage when we serve and its side-out percentage when we receive. On a serving point we keep serving. When we lose serve, we stay in that positional rotation and receive. After a successful side-out, we rotate to the next server.

Serve-in % divides serving rallies into missed serves and legal serves. Because point scoring already includes service errors, the probability of winning after a legal serve is calculated as point scoring % ÷ serve-in %. The app prevents impossible combinations where point scoring exceeds serve-in probability.

Earned side-out, first-ball side-out, and earned FBSO are editable diagnostic metrics. They do not enter the basic rally-win calculation because their events are already represented within side-out percentage. Their value is in understanding how the side-out percentage is achieved.

Starting server when receiving: the selected player is the first person who will serve after the opening side-out. That means the team begins receiving in the preceding positional rotation.

All six starting positions preserve the same circular serving order. The model uses pooled team statistics rather than a separate model of opponent rotations. Differences of a percentage point or two are not necessarily meaningful, especially given historical sampling uncertainty.

The serve-in rates in the baseline are provisional and should be checked against complete serving records; the rotation point scoring and side-out inputs are weighted 2026 Seattle + Portland observations. Results are estimates, not guarantees.

Privacy: simulations run entirely in your browser. No data is sent to a server. Saved scenarios use local device storage.
